# Pair 3 — Michigan RFP 260000000387, Youth Employment Permit Portal (LEO)

Source: the State's Award Letter and Evaluation Synopsis. Three bids; 80 of 100 technical points
needed. Criteria: Project Scope (Schedule A sections 4–16 and 21–26, Schedules D, E, G) 30;
Schedule A Attachment 1, Business Specifications Worksheet 40; Attachment 1 resumes 15; Vendor
Questions Worksheet 15.

| Bidder | Technical score | Result |
|---|---|---|
| EarnStride LLC | 89.5 / 100 | Award; best value: the most complete pre-existing system, least customisation, youth-employment domain expertise; price reduced by USD 3.3M in negotiation |
| Kunz, Leigh and Associates (KL&A) | 88.87 | Passed technical; USD 6.3M against EarnStride's 3.55M before negotiation |
| Concourse Tech Inc | disqualified | Did not use the required State RFP template, no Confidential Treatment Form, no Vendor Questions Worksheet |

## What the evaluators held against each bid

- **EarnStride** (4.5 of 15 on the Vendor Questions Worksheet): the three experience references
  were "not of expected size and scope" or "not fully in line with expectations". Everything
  else satisfactory.
- **KL&A** (24.1 of 30 on scope, 34.8 of 40 on the specifications): change-management process
  not explained (section 9, box 4); the bidder would generate its own SOW for future changes
  (9, box 5); meets "most but not all" requirements of an existing solution, calls a requirement
  "additional scope", and makes no commitment to the required go-live date (10, box 2);
  a third-party component (Aspose.Words) named without a cost (10, box 5); no list of compatible
  mobile devices or mobile features (10, boxes 7–8); training limited to train-the-trainer (14).
- **Concourse**: a free-form 37-page proposal instead of the State template. Reviewed here
  against the same RFP text as the others, it should show the missing worksheet answers as
  gaps across the board.

## What a correct review should show

1. Concourse far below the other two on completeness, with most requirements not addressed.
2. KL&A's timeline finding: no commitment to the go-live date, "additional scope" wording.
3. EarnStride's weakness confined to the experience references, otherwise the strongest.

**The State's order is reproduced**, and its reasons show up:

- Concourse's violation is the State's disqualification reason word for word, and its eleven
  missing requirements are the Vendor Questions Worksheet disclosures it never filled in
  (company history, debarment, litigation, sales, environmental and labour disclosures, the
  worksheet itself). A contradiction between accepting the State's terms (§2.38.1) and its own
  disclaimers (§2.40) is a genuine catch the State did not spell out.
- KL&A: the timeline weakness names the follow-up release "past the initial September go-live
  window into January 2027", which is the evaluators' "no commitment to hit our required
  go-live date"; the third-party component with its own pricing (the State's Aspose.Words
  remark) appears as a scope-creep finding; the unanswered risk column in the specifications
  worksheet is the risk weakness.
- EarnStride: one missing requirement (a named security officer), otherwise clean; the State's
  only deduction, the experience references, is not something the rubric measures.

What does not match the State: pricing scores 1 for all three because pricing sits in a separate
Schedule B file, and KL&A's low score is not as far below EarnStride's as the State's 40-point
worksheet difference would suggest.
